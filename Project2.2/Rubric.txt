3️⃣ Step 3: WordNet-Based Similarity Matrices
Steps to implement WordNet sentence similarity
Tokenize and POS-tag the sentences

Split each sentence into words and assign Penn Treebank POS tags using:

word_tokenize() and pos_tag() from nltk.

Convert POS tags to WordNet tags

Map the Penn tags to WordNet categories (noun, verb, adjective, adverb) using a helper function like penn_to_wn().

For tag "s", treat it as "a". Remove any tokens with tags other than those supported.
Retrieve WordNet synsets

For each word with a valid POS tag, obtain its first WordNet synset using:

wn.synsets() from nltk.corpus.wordnet.

Compute word similarity

For each pair of synsets from the two sentences, compute semantic similarity using:

path_similarity() from WordNet (nltk.corpus.wordnet).

Compute sentence similarity

Sum the valid similarity scores and normalize by dividing by the maximum number of synsets in the two sentences.

Make the similarity symmetric

Compute similarity in both directions and average the two scores 

Since sentence_similarity(s1, s2) and sentence_similarity(s2, s1) may produce different values, the implementation computes both and averages them:  (sim(s1, s2) + sim(s2, s1)) / 2

This ensures the similarity matrix is symmetric (the value for pair i,j equals j, i).

Build the similarity matrix

Compare every pair of sentences and store the results in an n × n matrix

Common WordNet tags:

n – Noun : Represents nouns, which are words that denote people, places, things, or ideas.
v – Verb : Represents verbs, which are words that describe actions or states.
a – Adjective : Represents adjectives, which are words that describe or modify nouns
r – Adverb : Represents adverbs, which are words that modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs and provide information about how, when, or where an action occurs.
s – Adjective satellite (treat as a) : Represents adjectives used as modifiers for nouns, often referred to as "adjective satellites" in WordNet.

WordNet preprocessing configurations:

With or without stopwords removal
With or without lemmatization
For each (configuration + WordNet), produce a similarity matrix.

Similarity method: Greedy Lemma Aligning Overlap (GLAO)

Result: 4 configurations × 1 method = 4 similarity matrices

4️⃣ Step 4: Compare NLP Matrices with Human Matrix

Total: 24 + 4 = 28 NLP similarity matrices

To compare each NLP matrix with the human matrix:

Extract half of each matrix (upper or lower triangle, excluding the diagonal).
Compute the Pearson correlation between the human matrix triangle and each NLP matrix triangle.
